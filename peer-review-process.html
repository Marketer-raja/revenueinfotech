Peer Review Process

Last updated: October 17, 2026

Our peer review is an editorial and technical review process carried out before and after publication. It is not academic peer review, and we do not claim that our articles are reviewed by outside scholars. It is a set of checks designed to catch errors and unsupported claims before they reach readers.

1. The Review Steps

  • Brief. Each piece begins with a defined topic, intended reader and list of sources to consult.
  • Drafting with sources. The author records the sources, versions and dates behind each material claim.
  • Technical verification. A second person with relevant knowledge checks procedures, code, configurations and numbers. Where practical, tutorial steps are re-run from scratch.
  • Editorial review. An editor checks clarity, balance, attribution, headline accuracy and required disclosures.
  • Publication. The piece is published with its date and, where relevant, the versions or test conditions it covers.
  • Post-publication review. Articles are revisited when products change or readers report problems, and updated or corrected as needed.

2. What We Check by Content Type

Content typeWhat the review covers
News and roundupsSource identified and linked; claim matches the source; clear difference between confirmed facts and speculation.
TutorialsSteps reproduced in a clean environment; software versions listed; commands and code checked; common errors noted.
ReviewsTesting method described; how the product was obtained disclosed; claims backed by observation rather than vendor material alone.
Comparisons and architecture analysesLike-for-like criteria; benchmark conditions stated; limits and trade-offs explained rather than a single winner declared without context.
Buying guidesSelection criteria explained; prices and availability dated; any commercial relationship disclosed.

3. Outside Experts

We may consult subject-matter experts for specialised topics. We name an expert only with their permission, and we do not imply an endorsement that has not been given.

4. Limits of Review

Technology changes quickly and review reduces errors but cannot remove them. Technical content is provided for general information, so test any procedure in a safe environment before using it in production.

5. Reader Feedback

Readers are part of our review. If a step fails, a number looks wrong or information is out of date, tell us through the Contact page. Confirmed issues are fixed and noted on the article. See our Editorial Ethics for how corrections are handled.
